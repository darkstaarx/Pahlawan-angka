# KSSR time notation policy

This is a display/content correction, not a change to mastery, routing, skill IDs, or curriculum coverage.

| Year | Curriculum evidence | Display rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | DSKP 5.2.3 analogue hours and fractional hours; Edisi 3 4.2 | Analogue read questions have no inferred AM/PM. Comparisons/stories supply the day-part. |
| 2 | Edisi 3 5.1.1 read/write, 5.1.2 record; DSKP 5.1.3 words/numerals | Contextual times use 12-hour Malay labels: pagi, tengah hari, petang, malam. No new 12/24 conversion task. |
| 3 | 5.1.1 schedules, 5.1.2 before/during/after activities | Schedules and story clock times include day-parts; duration arithmetic keeps units. |
| 4 | 4.1.1 relationship of 12-hour and 24-hour systems | Preserve the existing conversion exercises. 12-hour times identify day-part or a.m./p.m.; 24-hour times identify the system. |
| 5 | 4.1 duration, 4.2 unit relationships and arithmetic | Retain the existing task coverage using explicit inherited time notation; no new conversion standard. |
| 6 | Semakan 2017 4.1.1/4.1.2 time zones, 4.2.1 problems | Retain time-zone exercises, UTC offsets and day changes; explicitly identify 24-hour notation. |

The Year 2 word-to-numeral conversion is distinct from Year 4's 12-to-24-hour conversion. The older Year 6 KSSR standard 6.1 is not the current Semakan 2017 time-zone standard.

## Invariants

- A bare analogue clock cannot distinguish morning from evening.
- Clock arithmetic uses total minutes, then formats; no 13:00 in a 12-hour answer and no invalid minutes.
- Noon is 12:00 tengah hari / 12:00 p.m.; midnight is 12:00 tengah malam / 12:00 a.m.
- Durations (including 24 jam per day) never receive AM/PM.
- SVG/timeline labels and options use the question's chosen notation, and hidden endpoints remain hidden.
- No blanket text replacement of time-like strings: ratios, UTC offsets, durations, and calendar values retain their meaning.
